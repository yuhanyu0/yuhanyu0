# Hanyu Yu

**Mechanism identification under partial observation**

I study a simple but difficult question: **when different mechanisms look the same under ordinary measurements, what new observation or intervention first makes them distinguishable?**

My work uses controlled synthetic benchmarks, physical-network geometry, numerical solvers, market networks, and scientific-agent evaluation to separate **predictive regularity** from **mechanism identification**.

## Selected research

### [Mechanism Readability under Observation Interfaces](https://github.com/yuhanyu0/mechanism-readability)

A predictive morphological law can be real and held-out predictive without uniquely identifying its generator. This project combines empirical physical-network motifs with a controlled matched-mechanism benchmark to locate where additional path and tangent information restores distinguishability.

### [Coupling–Localization Atlas of Market Organization](https://github.com/yuhanyu0/coupling-localization-atlas)

Market correlation networks can show different leading-mode organizations at the same coupling rank. This NetSci 2026 project separates common-mode strength from localization and treats insufficiently visited coupling slices as not identifiable rather than absent.

### [Trace Is Not Uptake](https://github.com/yuhanyu0/trace-is-not-uptake)

Evidence appearing in an agent trace does not show that the evidence causally changed the output. This project develops paired interventions for separating evidence presence from causal uptake, with explicit scope boundaries between synthetic qualification and real-system evaluation.

## Research tools

### [Market State Observatory](https://github.com/yuhanyu0/market-state-observatory)

Auditable market evidence-state infrastructure that separates data readiness, model estimation, and decision eligibility.

## Research theme

> **Mechanism recovery and experimental discrimination under partial observation.**

Across domains, I am interested in the same progression:

**observation → response → intervention → mechanism distinction**

## Methods

Experimental design · network and spectral analysis · numerical optimization · agent-based modeling · counterfactual testing · reproducible scientific workflows
